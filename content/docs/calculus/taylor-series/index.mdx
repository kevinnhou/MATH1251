This chapter approximates a function near a point by a polynomial built from
its derivatives, bounds the error and then asks when the infinite version of
that polynomial equals the function. The finite part needs only
differentiation and integration by parts; the infinite part needs limits of
sequences and tests for the convergence of series, which the middle lessons
develop.

## Lessons

1. [Taylor polynomials](/calculus/taylor-series/taylor-polynomials): finite
   local models built from derivative data at one centre.
2. [Taylor's theorem](/calculus/taylor-series/taylors-theorem): the exact
   remainder, error bounds and the classification of stationary points.
3. [Sequences and limits](/calculus/taylor-series/sequences-and-limits): what
   it means for a sequence to converge and how to prove that it does.
4. [Infinite series](/calculus/taylor-series/infinite-series): infinite sums
   defined through their partial sums, with geometric, telescoping and
   harmonic examples.
5. [Convergence tests](/calculus/taylor-series/convergence-tests): comparison,
   integral, ratio, root and alternating tests for series with no closed sum.
6. [Taylor series](/calculus/taylor-series/taylor-series): the infinite
   extension of a Taylor polynomial and the remainder argument that decides
   when it equals the function.
7. [Power series](/calculus/taylor-series/power-series): series defined by
   their coefficients, their radius of convergence and termwise calculus
   inside that radius.
8. [Seen questions](/calculus/taylor-series/seen): past exam questions on
   Taylor coefficients, remainder bounds, sequence limits, convergence tests
   and power-series endpoints.

## Vocabulary checkpoints

- **Sequence and series:** a [sequence](/calculus/taylor-series/sequences-and-limits)
  is a list of terms; an [infinite series](/calculus/taylor-series/infinite-series)
  is defined by the limit of its partial sums.
- **Taylor polynomial and Taylor series:** a [Taylor
  polynomial](/calculus/taylor-series/taylor-polynomials) is finite, while a
  [Taylor series](/calculus/taylor-series/taylor-series) represents its
  function only where the remainder tends to zero.
- **Radius and interval of convergence:** the radius of a [power
  series](/calculus/taylor-series/power-series) fixes the open interval, but
  each endpoint must be tested separately.
- **Pointwise and uniform convergence:** pointwise convergence alone does not
  justify termwise differentiation; power series have the uniform control that
  does ([power series](/calculus/taylor-series/power-series)).
