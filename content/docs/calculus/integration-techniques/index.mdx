This chapter is a toolkit for common classes of integrals with elementary
antiderivatives, not a classification of every elementary integral. Each
lesson identifies the structure in the integrand that makes its method work,
so choose a method from that structure rather than from the presence of a
particular symbol. The chapter assumes substitution, integration by parts,
polynomial factorisation, completing the square and the standard
trigonometric identities.

## Lessons

1. [Trigonometric integrals](/calculus/integration-techniques/trigonometric-integrals):
   powers and products of sine, cosine, tangent and secant, using the
   Pythagorean, double-angle and product-to-sum identities.
2. [Reduction formulae](/calculus/integration-techniques/reduction-formulae):
   recurrences that organise repeated integration by parts when the same
   power appears with a lower index.
3. [Trigonometric and hyperbolic
   substitutions](/calculus/integration-techniques/trigonometric-and-hyperbolic-substitutions):
   clearing a quadratic surd by matching it to a Pythagorean identity.
4. [Rational functions](/calculus/integration-techniques/rational-functions):
   division and partial fractions, which lead to logarithms and inverse
   tangents.
5. [Rationalising
   substitutions](/calculus/integration-techniques/rationalising-substitutions):
   changes of variable that turn other surds and exponentials into rational
   functions.
6. [Seen questions](/calculus/integration-techniques/seen): past exam
   questions combining product-to-sum identities, reduction formulae,
   substitutions and partial fractions.

## Where it leads

The resulting antiderivatives are building blocks in [first-order
methods](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/first-order-equations) for
[ordinary differential equations](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations)
and in the iterated integrals of [Fubini's
theorem](/calculus/double-integrals/fubini-and-iterated-integrals).

## Vocabulary checkpoints

- **Antiderivative, indefinite and definite integral:** an antiderivative (or
  primitive) of $f$ is a function $F$ with $F'=f$; the indefinite integral
  $\int f(x)\,dx=F(x)+C$ is the family of them on an interval; the definite
  integral $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ is a number, equal to $F(b)-F(a)$ when $f$ is
  continuous on $[a,b]$ and $F'=f$ there.
- **Interval and constant:** every indefinite formula holds on a connected
  interval where the integrand is defined, so the integration constant may be
  chosen independently on different sides of a pole
  ([rational functions](/calculus/integration-techniques/rational-functions)).
- **Proper and improper:** an improper rational function needs division
  before partial fractions apply
  ([rational functions](/calculus/integration-techniques/rational-functions)).
- **Recurrence and antiderivative:** a reduction formula relates integrals in
  a family rather than evaluating one directly
  ([reduction formulae](/calculus/integration-techniques/reduction-formulae)).
- **Substitution and branch:** a substitution needs a real domain and a
  branch on which it is one-to-one and the branch fixes the sign of each
  square root
  ([trigonometric and hyperbolic substitutions](/calculus/integration-techniques/trigonometric-and-hyperbolic-substitutions)).
