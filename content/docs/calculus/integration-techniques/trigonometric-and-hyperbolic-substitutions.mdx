This page clears the quadratic surds $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$, $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$ and $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$. Each substitution matches a Pythagorean identity or its hyperbolic counterpart, so that the surd becomes a single trigonometric or hyperbolic function. Completing the square often produces one of these forms.

The sign pattern suggests the substitution, but the chosen branch decides which sign of the square root is valid. Right triangles help recover $\sin\theta$ and $\cos\theta$ when back-substituting, provided their side lengths are non-negative. On a branch where $x$ may be negative, use the identities together with the chosen signs instead.

Any powers of sine and cosine left over are [trigonometric integrals](/calculus/integration-techniques/trigonometric-integrals). Other surds may yield to a [rationalising substitution](/calculus/integration-techniques/rationalising-substitutions) that produces a [rational function](/calculus/integration-techniques/rational-functions), although not every surd has such a simple substitution.

## Choosing a trigonometric or hyperbolic substitution

<Method slug="trig-hyperbolic-substitution">

### Substitutions for $\sqrt{a^2\pm x^2}$ and $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$

<Statement>

For $a>0$, choose the indicated trigonometric or hyperbolic branch so that
the substitution is one-to-one on the interval being integrated. Keep the
absolute value when simplifying a square root until the chosen range fixes
its sign; for example,
$\sqrt{a^2-a^2\sin^2\theta}=a|\cos\theta|$.

| Integrand factor | Substitutions | Identities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$ | $x=a\sin\theta$, $dx=a\cos\theta\,d\theta$ or $x=a\tanh\theta$, $dx=a\operatorname{sech}^2\theta\,d\theta$ | $\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1$, $\operatorname{sech}^2\theta+\tanh^2\theta=1$ |
| $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$ | $x=a\tan\theta$, $dx=a\sec^2\theta\,d\theta$ or $x=a\sinh\theta$, $dx=a\cosh\theta\,d\theta$ | $\sec^2\theta=\tan^2\theta+1$, $\cosh^2\theta=1+\sinh^2\theta$ |
| $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$ | $x=a\sec\theta$, $dx=a\sec\theta\tan\theta\,d\theta$ or, for $x\ge a$, $x=a\cosh\theta$, $dx=a\sinh\theta\,d\theta$ | $\sec^2\theta=1+\tan^2\theta$, $\cosh^2\theta=1+\sinh^2\theta$ |

</Statement>

For $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$ the hypotenuse is $a$ and the side opposite $\theta$ is $x$, so the adjacent side is $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$. For $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$ the opposite side is $x$, the adjacent side is $a$ and the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$. For $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$, this triangle description assumes $x\ge a$: the hypotenuse is $x$, the adjacent side is $a$ and the opposite side is $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$.

The identity $\operatorname{sech}^2\theta+\tanh^2\theta=1$ is the same relation as $1-\tanh^2\theta=\operatorname{sech}^2\theta$.

Writing $\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta}=\cos\theta$ without the absolute value is legitimate on the range of $\sin^{-1}$, where $\cos\theta\ge 0$; it fails if $\theta$ is taken outside that range. For $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$ on $x\le-a$, use a separate branch, such as $x=-a\cosh\theta$ with $\theta\ge0$, or choose a secant range and retain the required signs.

</Method>

## Trigonometric substitutions

<Example slug="int-sqrt-one-minus-x2" of="trig-hyperbolic-substitution" see="trig-even-power" difficulty="exam">

### $\int\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx$

The integrand is real on $[-1,1]$. On the interior of this interval, let
$x=\sin\theta$, so $dx=\cos\theta\,d\theta$. On the range of $\sin^{-1}$, one has $\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta}=\lvert\cos\theta\rvert=\cos\theta$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
I&=\int\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta}\,\cos\theta\,d\theta\\
&=\int\cos^2\theta\,d\theta\\
&=\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{\sin 2\theta}{4}+C\\
&=\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{\sin\theta\cos\theta}{2}+C\\
&=\frac12\sin^{-1}x+\frac12 x\sqrt{1-x^2}+C.
\end{aligned}
$$

The integral $\int\cos^2\theta\,d\theta$ is the [even-power evaluation](/calculus/integration-techniques/trigonometric-integrals). Back-substitution uses $\sin 2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta$, $\theta=\sin^{-1}x$, $\sin\theta=x$ and $\cos\theta=\sqrt{1-x^2}$.

</Example>

<Example slug="int-x2-sqrt-16-minus-x2" of="trig-hyperbolic-substitution" difficulty="exam">

### $\int_0^4 x^2\sqrt{16-x^2}\,dx$

Let $x=4\sin\theta$, so $dx=4\cos\theta\,d\theta$. The limits $x=0$ and $x=4$ become $\theta=0$ and $\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}$. On $\bigl[0,\frac{\pi}{2}\bigr]$ one has $\sqrt{16-16\sin^2\theta}=\sqrt{16(1-\sin^2\theta)}=4\cos\theta$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
I&=\int_0^{\pi/2}16\sin^2\theta\,\sqrt{16-16\sin^2\theta}\,4\cos\theta\,d\theta\\
&=\int_0^{\pi/2}16\sin^2\theta\cdot 4\cos\theta\cdot 4\cos\theta\,d\theta\\
&=16^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta\\
&=16^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\left(\frac{\sin 2\theta}{2}\right)^2\,d\theta\\
&=\frac{16^2}{4}\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{1-\cos 4\theta}{2}\,d\theta\\
&=32\int_0^{\pi/2}(1-\cos 4\theta)\,d\theta\\
&=32\left[\theta-\frac{\sin 4\theta}{4}\right]_0^{\pi/2}\\
&=32\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\sin 2\pi}{4}\right)\\
&=16\pi.
\end{aligned}
$$

The calculation uses $\sin\theta\cos\theta=\frac{\sin 2\theta}{2}$ and $\sin^2 2\theta=\frac{1-\cos 4\theta}{2}$.

</Example>

<Example slug="int-a2-plus-x2-three-halves" of="trig-hyperbolic-substitution" difficulty="exam">

### $\int\frac{dx}{(a^2+x^2)^{3/2}}$

Assume $a>0$ and let $x=a\tan\theta$ with
$-\frac{\pi}{2}<\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}$, so
$dx=a\sec^2\theta\,d\theta$ and $\sec\theta>0$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
I&=\int\frac{a\sec^2\theta}{(a^2+a^2\tan^2\theta)^{3/2}}\,d\theta\\
&=\frac{1}{a^2}\int\frac{\sec^2\theta}{(1+\tan^2\theta)^{3/2}}\,d\theta\\
&=\frac{1}{a^2}\int\frac{\sec^2\theta}{\sec^3\theta}\,d\theta\\
&=\frac{1}{a^2}\int\frac{1}{\sec\theta}\,d\theta\\
&=\frac{1}{a^2}\int\cos\theta\,d\theta\\
&=\frac{1}{a^2}\sin\theta+C\\
&=\frac{x}{a^2\sqrt{x^2+a^2}}+C.
\end{aligned}
$$

The identity $\sec^2\theta=\tan^2\theta+1$ gives $(1+\tan^2\theta)^{3/2}=\sec^3\theta$ because $\sec\theta>0$ on the chosen branch. Also $\cos\theta=a/\sqrt{x^2+a^2}$, so $\sin\theta=\tan\theta\cos\theta=x/\sqrt{x^2+a^2}$ even when $x<0$.

</Example>

## Hyperbolic substitutions

The same surds can be cleared by a hyperbolic identity. On the $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$ row, $x=a\sinh\theta$ uses $\cosh^2\theta=1+\sinh^2\theta$ in place of $\sec^2\theta=1+\tan^2\theta$, so the surd becomes $a\cosh\theta$ instead of $a\sec\theta$.

<Example slug="int-one-over-sqrt-4-plus-x2-hyperbolic" of="trig-hyperbolic-substitution" difficulty="routine">

### $\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{4+x^2}}$

Let $x=2\sinh\theta$, so $dx=2\cosh\theta\,d\theta$. Then $\sqrt{4+x^2}=\sqrt{4+4\sinh^2\theta}=2\cosh\theta$, using $\cosh^2\theta-\sinh^2\theta=1$ with $\cosh\theta>0$. Hence

$$
I=\int\frac{2\cosh\theta}{2\cosh\theta}\,d\theta=\theta+C=\sinh^{-1}\!\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+C.
$$

The trigonometric route on the same row, $x=2\tan\theta$, leads to $\int\sec\theta\,d\theta$ instead.

</Example>
