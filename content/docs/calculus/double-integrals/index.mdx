A double integral $\iint_\Omega f(x,y)\,dA$ accumulates a quantity over a
planar region $\Omega$ by adding contributions of the form
$\text{density}\times\text{area}$. Depending on what $f$ represents, the result
is a signed volume, a mass, a population or a probability. The chapter assumes
one-variable integration and the
[multivariable function](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/geometry-and-approximation)
viewpoint.

## Lessons

1. [Double integrals and Riemann sums](/calculus/double-integrals/double-integrals-and-riemann-sums):
   defines the integral over a rectangle as a limit of sums and gives the
   upper-and-lower-sum test for integrability.
2. [Fubini and iterated integrals](/calculus/double-integrals/fubini-and-iterated-integrals):
   computes the integral on a rectangle by adding one-variable cross-sections,
   in either order.
3. [Regions and order of integration](/calculus/double-integrals/regions-and-order-of-integration):
   extends slicing to regions bounded by curves and shows how to reverse or
   split the order.
4. [Change of variables and polar coordinates](/calculus/double-integrals/change-of-variables-and-polar-coordinates):
   reshapes the region or integrand, with the Jacobian determinant recording
   how area stretches.
5. [Mass, centroids and probability](/calculus/double-integrals/mass-centroids-and-probability):
   integrates a density to find mass, centres of mass and expected values.
6. [Euler's Basel sum](/calculus/double-integrals/basel-sum): combines a
   series, a linear change of variables and symmetry to prove
   $\sum 1/n^2=\pi^2/6$.
7. [Seen questions](/calculus/double-integrals/seen): exam questions on
   integrability, upper sums and a change of variables on an elliptical region.

## Where it leads

This last Calculus chapter extends the substitution rule
$du=g'(x)\,dx$ from [integration techniques](/calculus/integration-techniques)
to two variables; the absolute Jacobian determinant is also the area-scaling
factor of a [linear map's
matrix](/algebra/linear-transformations/matrix-representations) in Algebra.
Normalised densities turn the same integrals into continuous probability
models.

## Vocabulary checkpoints

- **Double and iterated integrals:** a [double
  integral](/calculus/double-integrals/double-integrals-and-riemann-sums)
  accumulates over a region; an [iterated
  integral](/calculus/double-integrals/fubini-and-iterated-integrals) computes
  it by slicing.
- **Order and region:** [changing the
  order](/calculus/double-integrals/regions-and-order-of-integration) changes
  the bounds, not the region.
- **Jacobian matrix and determinant:** in a [coordinate
  change](/calculus/double-integrals/change-of-variables-and-polar-coordinates),
  the matrix is the local linear approximation and the absolute value of its
  determinant is the area-scaling factor.
- **Density and integral:** in [mass and
  probability](/calculus/double-integrals/mass-centroids-and-probability), a
  density is a rate per unit area; its integral is the total mass or
  probability.
- **Region notation:** $D$ usually denotes a function's domain, $R$ a rectangle
  and $\Omega$ the region of integration. $\partial\Omega$ is the boundary of
  $\Omega$, while $\partial$ applied to a function means partial
  differentiation.
