This chapter extends differentiation to functions $f:D\to\mathbb{R}$ with
$D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$, whose inputs can move in many directions. Gradients,
tangent planes and local polynomial models describe such functions and turn
optimisation, with or without constraints, into systems of equations. The
chapter assumes one-variable differentiation and [Taylor
polynomials](/calculus/taylor-series/taylor-polynomials).

## Lessons

1. [Derivatives and level-set
   geometry](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/geometry-and-approximation):
   partial derivatives, the gradient as a vector of rates and a normal to level
   sets and tangent planes.
2. [Multivariable
   approximation](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/multivariable-approximation):
   the tangent plane as a first-order Taylor model, the Hessian and Taylor
   polynomials through total degree three.
3. [Local extrema](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/local-extrema):
   critical and stationary points and the Hessian test for minima, maxima and
   saddles.
4. [Global extrema](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/global-extrema):
   the extreme-value theorem and the interior-and-boundary search on a closed
   and bounded region.
5. [Constrained
   extrema](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/constrained-extrema):
   Lagrange multipliers for an equality constraint, applied to distance
   problems.
6. [Economic
   applications](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/economic-applications):
   maximising production under a budget, including Cobb-Douglas allocation.
7. [Seen questions](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/seen): past exam
   questions on partial derivatives of composite functions and the
   classification of critical points.

## Vocabulary checkpoints

- **Gradient and total derivative:** the gradient is the vector of partial
  derivatives; the total derivative is the linear map
  $\mathbf{h}\mapsto\nabla f\cdot\mathbf{h}$ that it represents at a
  differentiable point ([derivatives and level-set
  geometry](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/geometry-and-approximation)).
- **Partial derivatives and differentiability:** partial derivatives can exist
  at a point where $f$ is not differentiable, or not even continuous ([local
  extrema](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/local-extrema)).
- **Stationary and critical points:** a stationary point has zero gradient; a
  critical point is an interior point that is either stationary or a point
  where $f$ is not differentiable ([local
  extrema](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/local-extrema)).
- **Local and global extrema:** a local extremum compares nearby values; a
  global extremum on a closed and bounded region also needs every boundary
  candidate ([global
  extrema](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/global-extrema)).
- **Parallel and equal gradients:** the Lagrange condition
  $\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g$ says that the gradients are parallel, not equal,
  and it is guaranteed only where $\nabla g\ne\mathbf{0}$ ([constrained
  extrema](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/constrained-extrema)).
