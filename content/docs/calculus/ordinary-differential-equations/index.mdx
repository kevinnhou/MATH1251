An ordinary differential equation (ODE) describes a function through how it
changes and the task is to reconstruct the functions that satisfy it. ODEs
model motion, population change, cooling, mixing and oscillation. The chapter
assumes the differentiation, integration and algebra of the earlier calculus
material.

## Methods by structure

Each exact method relies on a structure the equation must have:

- **Separable:** separate the variables and integrate.
- **First-order linear:** an integrating factor turns the left side into a
  product-rule derivative.
- **Exact:** the equation is the total differential of a potential.
- **Substitution:** homogeneous and Bernoulli substitutions change variables
  into one of the cases above.
- **Second-order constant-coefficient:** an algebraic equation gives the
  exponential modes.

When no structure applies, a numerical method follows the slope field instead.

## Vocabulary checkpoints

- **Order and degree:** the order is the highest derivative present and the
  degree, when the equation is a polynomial in its derivatives, is the power
  of that derivative ([ODE fundamentals](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/ode-fundamentals)).
- **Solution formula and domain:** a formula solves an ODE only on the
  interval or branch where it is defined and satisfies the equation
  ([ODE fundamentals](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/ode-fundamentals)).
- **Separable, linear and exact:** these are independent tests, so one
  equation can pass several of them
  ([first-order methods](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/first-order-equations)).
- **Equilibrium and integration constant:** an equilibrium is a constant
  solution, while a constant of integration labels a member of the solution
  family
  ([modelling](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/first-order-modelling)).
- **Homogeneous and particular solutions:** the homogeneous part carries the
  arbitrary constants and the particular part accounts for the forcing
  ([second-order equations](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/second-order-constant-coefficient-equations)).
- **Local and global error:** the local error comes from one numerical step,
  while the global error also includes errors carried forward from earlier
  steps
  ([numerical solutions](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/numerical-solutions)).
