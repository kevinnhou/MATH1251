Calculus studies change, approximation and accumulation. The strand assumes
familiar differentiation, substitution and integration by parts and builds
from integration and differential equations to series and functions of several
variables.

## Chapters

1. [Integration techniques](/calculus/integration-techniques): choosing a
   method from the form of the integrand. Substitutions change the variable,
   trigonometric identities change the expression and reduction formulae
   relate a family of integrals.
2. [Ordinary differential equations](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations):
   [first-order methods](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/first-order-equations)
   such as separation and integrating factors, second-order
   constant-coefficient equations, [when solutions exist and are
   unique](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/existence-uniqueness-and-linear-operators),
   and [numerical solutions](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/numerical-solutions)
   with error control.
3. [Taylor series](/calculus/taylor-series): Taylor polynomials and remainder
   bounds, then sequences, series and convergence. A Taylor series represents
   its function only when the remainder tends to zero.
4. [Functions of several variables](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables):
   gradients as level-set normals and linear models, the Hessian test at
   interior stationary points where it applies, boundary checks for global
   extrema and Lagrange multipliers for equality constraints.
5. [Double integrals](/calculus/double-integrals): accumulation over planar
   regions using slices and changes of variables, applied to area, mass and
   probability.

## Links to Algebra

- **Polynomial spaces:** Taylor polynomials live in the [polynomial vector
  spaces](/algebra/vector-spaces/vector-spaces) of Algebra.
- **Kernels:** the solutions of a homogeneous linear ODE form the
  [kernel](/algebra/linear-transformations/kernel-image-rank) of a linear
  differential operator; every forced solution is one particular solution plus
  an element of that kernel.
- **Characteristic roots:** complex characteristic roots describe exponential
  growth, decay and oscillation, read through [polar
  form](/algebra/complex-numbers/polar-form). Coupled equations use the
  eigenvectors of [linear ODE systems](/algebra/eigenvalues/linear-ode-systems).

See [Core](/core) for how the two strands fit together.
