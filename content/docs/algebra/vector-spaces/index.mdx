A vector space is the setting in which linear combinations make sense. This
chapter studies which subsets inherit that structure, which vectors a chosen
set produces and when the coefficients are unique. It assumes row reduction
of linear systems and works over $\mathbb R$ unless stated otherwise; every
span, independence and dimension claim depends on the chosen scalar field.

## Lessons

1. [Vector spaces](/algebra/vector-spaces/vector-spaces): the axioms, their
   first consequences and the coordinate, polynomial and matrix examples.
2. [Subspaces](/algebra/vector-spaces/subspaces): subsets that inherit the
   operations, tested by closure.
3. [Spans](/algebra/vector-spaces/spans): every linear combination of chosen
   vectors, with membership decided by a linear system.
4. [Linear independence](/algebra/vector-spaces/linear-independence): when no
   vector is redundant, so coefficients are unique.
5. [Basis and dimension](/algebra/vector-spaces/basis-and-dimension):
   independent spanning sets and the number of vectors they contain.
6. [Coordinates and bases](/algebra/vector-spaces/coordinates-and-bases):
   building bases from data and recording coefficients as coordinate vectors.
7. [Data fitting](/algebra/vector-spaces/data-fitting): least-squares lines,
   and exact polynomial interpolation as a coordinate problem.
8. [Seen questions](/algebra/vector-spaces/seen): past exam questions across
   coordinate, polynomial, function and matrix spaces, with later-topic
   previews marked.

## Where it leads

[Linear transformations](/algebra/linear-transformations) are the maps that
preserve linear combinations. Once bases are chosen, coordinates turn such a
map into a matrix.

## Vocabulary checkpoints

- A [span](/algebra/vector-spaces/spans) is a subspace; a spanning set is a
  list of vectors that generates it.
- [Independence](/algebra/vector-spaces/linear-independence) makes
  coefficients unique; a [basis](/algebra/vector-spaces/basis-and-dimension)
  is also required to span.
- [Coordinates](/algebra/vector-spaces/coordinates-and-bases) depend on the
  chosen ordered basis; the vector itself does not.
- [Interpolation](/algebra/vector-spaces/data-fitting) fits the data exactly,
  while least squares only approximates it.
