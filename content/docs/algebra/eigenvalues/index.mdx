This chapter looks for a basis in which a
[matrix representation](/algebra/linear-transformations/matrix-representations)
becomes diagonal, so that the map simply scales each basis direction. Such a
basis turns matrix powers, coupled differential equations and Markov chains
into independent scalar modes. The chapter assumes linear maps, kernels and
matrices and reads growth and decay using
[stability of systems](/algebra/complex-numbers/stability-of-systems).

## Lessons

1. [Eigenvalues and eigenvectors](/algebra/eigenvalues/eigenvalues-and-eigenvectors):
   directions that a matrix scales, found as roots of the characteristic
   polynomial.
2. [Diagonalisation](/algebra/eigenvalues/diagonalisation): when the
   eigenvectors form an [eigenbasis](/algebra/eigenvalues/diagonalisation),
   giving $A=PDP^{-1}$.
3. [Spectral applications](/algebra/eigenvalues/spectral-applications):
   multiplicities, matrix powers and the long-term behaviour of each mode.
4. [Linear ODE systems](/algebra/eigenvalues/linear-ode-systems): solving
   $\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}$ with exponential modes in place of powers.
5. [Markov systems](/algebra/eigenvalues/markov-systems): transition
   matrices, stationary distributions and long-run limits.
6. [Seen questions](/algebra/eigenvalues/seen): exam problems on stability,
   diagonalisation, ODE systems and Markov models.

## Where it leads

This is the last Algebra chapter. Its exponential modes match the
characteristic roots of [second-order constant-coefficient
equations](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/second-order-constant-coefficient-equations),
and the eigenvalues of a Hessian give the curvature at [stationary
points](/calculus/functions-of-several-variables/local-extrema) in Calculus.

## Vocabulary checkpoints

- An [eigenvalue, eigenvector and eigenspace](/algebra/eigenvalues/eigenvalues-and-eigenvectors)
  are a scalar, a non-zero vector and a subspace respectively.
- [Algebraic and geometric multiplicity](/algebra/eigenvalues/spectral-applications)
  count different things and comparing them shows whether
  [diagonalisation](/algebra/eigenvalues/diagonalisation) is possible.
- In [Markov systems](/algebra/eigenvalues/markov-systems), a transition
  matrix, a probability vector and a stationary distribution are three
  different objects.
