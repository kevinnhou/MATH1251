A linear transformation, or linear map, is a function between vector spaces
that preserves addition and scalar multiplication. This chapter reads such
maps through their geometry, their kernel and image and, in finite
dimensions, as matrices once bases are chosen. It assumes the bases,
dimension and coordinates of [vector spaces](/algebra/vector-spaces).

## Lessons

1. [Linear transformations](/algebra/linear-transformations/linear-transformations):
   the definition, tests for linearity and why values on a basis determine
   the whole map.
2. [Geometric examples](/algebra/linear-transformations/geometric-examples):
   matrix maps in standard coordinates, read as scalings, reflections,
   rotations and projections.
3. [Kernel, image and rank](/algebra/linear-transformations/kernel-image-rank):
   the inputs a map sends to zero, the outputs it can produce and their
   dimensions.
4. [Rank-nullity](/algebra/linear-transformations/rank-nullity): how nullity
   and rank add up to the dimension of the domain and what this says about
   the solutions of $Ax=b$.
5. [Matrix representations](/algebra/linear-transformations/matrix-representations):
   the matrix of a map relative to chosen bases and injectivity,
   surjectivity and invertibility read from that matrix.
6. [Seen questions](/algebra/linear-transformations/seen): past exam problems
   that combine these views in longer arguments.

## Where it leads

Once a map has a matrix, the [eigenvalues chapter](/algebra/eigenvalues) asks
whether a different basis can make that matrix diagonal, so that the map acts
independently along invariant directions. In Calculus, the kernel of a
[linear differential
operator](/calculus/ordinary-differential-equations/existence-uniqueness-and-linear-operators)
describes every solution of a homogeneous linear ODE.

## Vocabulary checkpoints

- **Map and matrix:** a [linear
  transformation](/algebra/linear-transformations/linear-transformations) is
  a map; its [matrix
  representation](/algebra/linear-transformations/matrix-representations)
  depends on the chosen bases.
- **Kernel and image:** the
  [kernel](/algebra/linear-transformations/kernel-image-rank) is a subspace of
  the domain, while the image is a subspace of the codomain.
- **Nullity and rank:** [nullity and
  rank](/algebra/linear-transformations/rank-nullity) are the dimensions of
  the kernel and the image and they add up to the dimension of the domain.
- **Injective and surjective:** a linear map is
  [injective](/algebra/linear-transformations/matrix-representations) when its
  kernel is trivial and surjective when its image is the whole codomain.
- **Linear map and operator:** an
  [operator](/algebra/linear-transformations/linear-transformations) is a
  linear map from a space to itself and eigenvalues are defined only for
  operators.
