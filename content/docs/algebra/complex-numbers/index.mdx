This chapter builds the complex numbers $\mathbb C$, the number system in
which every polynomial has a root and multiplication acts as rotation and
scaling. Those roots later describe the growth, decay and oscillation of
recurrences and differential equations. The chapter assumes only real
arithmetic and basic trigonometry.

## Lessons

1. [Complex arithmetic](/algebra/complex-numbers/complex-arithmetic):
   Cartesian form $a+bi$, conjugation, modulus, the Argand plane and the field
   axioms.
2. [Polar form](/algebra/complex-numbers/polar-form): modulus and argument,
   products as rotations, De Moivre's formula and $n$th roots.
3. [Trigonometric and geometric
   applications](/algebra/complex-numbers/trigonometric-and-geometric-applications):
   trigonometric identities from exponential forms and loci in the Argand
   plane.
4. [Complex polynomials](/algebra/complex-numbers/complex-polynomials): roots,
   factors and multiplicity, the fundamental theorem of algebra and real
   factorisation through conjugate pairs.
5. [Stability of systems](/algebra/complex-numbers/stability-of-systems):
   characteristic roots as modes of recurrences and differential equations,
   and the root tests for stability.
6. [Seen questions](/algebra/complex-numbers/seen): past exam questions on
   complex roots, factorisation and root counts.

## Vocabulary checkpoints

- **Number and point:** the Argand plane pictures $a+bi$ as the point $(a,b)$;
  the picture represents the number but does not replace its algebra
  ([complex arithmetic](/algebra/complex-numbers/complex-arithmetic)).
- **Modulus and argument:** the modulus is a size; an argument is a direction,
  determined only up to multiples of $2\pi$
  ([polar form](/algebra/complex-numbers/polar-form)).
- **Roots, factors and multiplicity:** a root $\alpha$ gives a factor
  $z-\alpha$ and multiplicity counts how often that factor repeats
  ([complex polynomials](/algebra/complex-numbers/complex-polynomials)).
- **Discrete and continuous tests:** a mode $r^n$ decays when $|r|<1$, while a
  mode $e^{rt}$ decays when $\operatorname{Re}(r)<0$
  ([stability of systems](/algebra/complex-numbers/stability-of-systems)).
- **Stable and bounded:** in these notes *stable* means every solution decays
  to zero, so bounded solutions that do not decay are a separate boundary case
  ([stability of systems](/algebra/complex-numbers/stability-of-systems)).
